    Choose a PCB Design¶

    There are two separate printed circuit board (PCB) designs for the Z80 ROMless SBC. Both implement the proven breadboard's assisted-loader circuit, but use different component packages, SRAM parts and assembly methods.

    The dual in-line package (DIP) / through-hole PCB is intended for hand assembly with socketed ICs. The partial surface-mount technology (SMT) PCB is intended for JLCPCB assembly of the surface-mount parts, leaving four items to hand-solder. It is not an all-SMT board: the Z80 remains a DIP-40 device.

    Compare the two designs¶

    Best suited to One hand-built machine, lower assembly outlay and easy IC replacement A compact board with factory-assembled SMT parts
    Board 85 x 96 mm, four layers, 1.6 mm FR-4 98.3 x 60 mm, four layers, 1.6 mm FR-4
    KiCad project directory hardware/kicad/ hardware/kicad-smt/
    Z80 Z84C0020PEC, socketed DIP-40 Z84C0020PEC, hand-soldered DIP-40
    SRAM AS6C1008-55PCN, DIP-32, 55 ns IS62C1024AL-35QLI, SOP-32, 35 ns
    Logic ICs Socketed DIP packages JLC-assembled SOIC packages
    Pico 2 W Plug-in headers/socket strips JLC-reflowed castellated module
    Pull resistors Six bussed arrays, 40 branches Forty individual 10k 0603 resistors
    USB-C power entry Six-pin breakout module Onboard receptacle and two 5.1k CC pull-downs
    Assembly Hand assembly Top-side SMT assembly plus Z80, OLED socket and J2/J3

    Choose the documentation and manufacturing package for the same design. The two BOMs, footprints, board outlines and placement files are not interchangeable.

    Building just one machine¶

    The DIP design is the budget-oriented choice if you want one working board and are willing to solder it yourself. Populate one PCB and keep the other bare boards as spares; there are no factory assembly, stencil or component-loading charges.

    JLCPCB's standard bare-PCB order starts at five boards for either design, not only for SMT. The extra cost of the partial-SMT option is the factory assembly and its associated charges, in addition to the PCBs and components. The number of bare PCBs manufactured and the number assembled are separate order settings; do not assume that a five-PCB order requires all five to be populated.

    For multiple machines, factory assembly spreads setup costs and reduces hand-soldering work. Compare the complete current quote, including components, assembly, shipping and taxes, rather than just the bare-PCB price. Check JLCPCB's live quote for quantities and its assembly price breakdown for the applicable charges.

    Shared circuit, separate qualification¶

    Both boards retain the same Pico GPIO assignments, Z80-only address bus, buffered A16 bank selection, fixed HCT32 logic and assisted SRAM loading. Both provide a 3.3 V OLED connection and use GND/+5V inner planes. The shared pin map describes the signal connections; each board's inventory specifies the actual package and part number.

    The ISSI SRAM on the SMT board has the same SOP-32 pin assignment as the Alliance DIP SRAM, but different voltage thresholds and timing limits. Its 35 ns access time does not by itself qualify the board at 20 MHz.

    PCB qualification is separate from the breadboard

    The Phase 0-10 build plan describes the three-BB830 breadboard prototype, not either PCB's assembly procedure. Each PCB needs its own power, assembly and timing checks, starting at 1 MHz. Neither a passing breadboard nor clean ERC/DRC reports establish operation of an assembled PCB at the 20 MHz target.
